# The org-wide AI maturity ladder

Five levels from scattered experiments to AI built into how the work gets done, with the training each level needs. For leaders, HR and L&D teams responsible for AI adoption across the whole organization.

Read it with commentary: https://steeltownai.io/blog/org-ai-maturity-ladder
5-page PDF: [carousels/org-ai-maturity-ladder.pdf](../carousels/org-ai-maturity-ladder.pdf)

## Why it matters

- **36%** of employees feel adequately upskilled for AI, while 72% say the skills they need have shifted. (BCG AI at Work, 2026, ~12,000 people)
- **66%** of office professionals have used AI tools at work that they believed company policy did not allow. (PagerDuty and Wakefield, a vendor survey, June 2026)
- **62%** of 721 firms sit in the first two of four AI maturity stages, where financial performance is below industry average. (MIT CISR, Dec 2024)

## 1. Explore

People try AI on their own, often on personal accounts, and nobody knows what goes in.

- List the AI tools in use, including what is paid on cards and expense reports.
- Publish a one-page policy: approved tools, what never goes in, and who checks output.

**Working when:** Everyone can name the approved tools and the never-paste list.

## 2. Equip

Everyone gets approved tools and hands-on training on their own tasks, not a generic demo.

- Run a 60-minute session per team, built on three of that team’s real tasks.
- Start a shared prompt library per role, kept where people already work.

**Working when:** Weekly active users of approved tools rise, not just seat count.

## 3. Standardize

Each team agrees on the best way to do its recurring AI tasks and writes it down.

- Name one champion per team, with an hour a week to help others.
- Turn each team’s top five recurring tasks into written workflows: the prompt, the model, and how the output gets checked.

**Working when:** Someone new can follow a team’s AI workflows on day one.

## 4. Automate

The highest-volume written workflows become agents connected to the systems they need.

- Train the builders, often ops staff and analysts, on agent tools, test sets and spend limits.
- Take each one through the agent rollout ladder: test set, shadow mode, guardrails, then scale.

**Working when:** Each agent in production has an owner, a test set and a budget.

## 5. Compound

Agents and people share one store of company knowledge, and AI results are reviewed like any other investment.

- Keep a shared company wiki that every AI tool reads, with a named owner for each page.
- Review AI spend and results together every quarter, and retire the workflows that do not pay.

**Working when:** AI spend and outcomes are reviewed in the same meeting.

## Track every month

- Share of staff active each week on approved tools
- Share of staff trained on their own tasks
- Workflows written down, by team
- Agents in production, each with an owner
- Cost per task on the top workflows
